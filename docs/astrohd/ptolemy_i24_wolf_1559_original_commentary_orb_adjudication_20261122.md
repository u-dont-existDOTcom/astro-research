## Original Greek/Latin first printing independently inspected

The Bavarian Academy's [Ptolemaeus Arabus et Latinus digitization](https://ptolemaeus.badw.de/print/11/2/51) indexes **Hieronymus Wolf's 1559 Basel printed Greek edition with facing Latin translation** of the *Anonymous Commentary on Ptolemy's Tetrabiblos*. Original printed **pages 50, 51 and 52** were obtained and directly read as full page images, including the relevant paragraphs under **“Modo ne longo intervallo distent.”** on **p.51**. The original high-resolution page images are preserved in the authorized owner-private historical-source archive, never bundled into the public repository.

**Original page 51, Latin (brief exact source segments):** **“Ne altera sit in 30 signi parte, altera in prima”** and **“sed ad minimum à 15. partibus”**; the passage immediately qualifies the distance using the planets' **“tardarum, tum velocium”** characteristics. The Greek column, printed alongside, likewise refers to fifteen parts and the nature of faster and slower stars. These are not separate directions for all planets with identical speed.

A bounded reading is therefore: the commentator discusses a distance of about **15 zodiacal parts in context**, contrasts bodies near opposite edges of a sign, **and qualifies that discussion by the planets' differing speeds**. The relationship of the printed Latin “ad minimum” to Robbins's later compressed **maximum** requires trained philological review of the original Greek and the manuscript witnesses. **This does not justify adopting 15° as an independent, universally valid upper bound** or replacing prior 1/3/5/10/15 sensitivity counts with a fabricated unique fifth-mode cutoff.

The preceding **p.50** comments that even intervals of **two, three or five parts** can describe an applying configuration under the stated slower/faster conditions; this contextual passage again argues against a fully uniform numerical application rule for all planetary pairs. The adjacent p.52 was inspected to keep the boundaries of the discussion and the commentary attribution grounded.

## Independent bibliographic cross-check and witness limit

The official [Ptolemaeus Arabus et Latinus work register](https://ptolemaeus.badw.de/work/242) identifies Wolf's 1559 edition as the **first printing of the Greek text**, prepared from **Munich BSB, Cod. Graec. 59**, with Wolf's Latin rendering. It also says that a critical edition is in preparation; in this research pass, **no completed modern critical collation of the paragraph has been located or substituted for a direct reading**. Raúl Caballero-Sánchez's peer-reviewed [2022 analysis](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/721845) distinguishes the ancient anonymous commentary from Wolf's much later printing; the article also discusses the original textual-transmission problem. An original *printed* page is a verified witness, not proof that Wolf's particular manuscript contains the only historically correct wording. The printed Greek column was **visually inspected, not independently fully translated against every surviving manuscript**.

This is a materially different evidential chain:

1. **Ptolemy, Tetrabiblos I.24 (original Greek/Robbins 1940 Loeb):** the interval for application/separation must be *not great*; **no universal numerical orb supplied in the cited original passage**.
2. **Anonymous historical commentator, as preserved by Wolf's 1559 Greek/Latin print, original p.51:** discusses approximately fifteen zodiacal parts and explicitly qualifies by planetary speed; source interpretation of its minimum/maximum language is unresolved.
3. **Robbins, Loeb editor, I.24 note 129:** reports the anonymous commentator as a **15° maximum**; this is an editorial report, not a new Ptolemaic rule.
4. **Modern astronomical calculations:** 1/3/5/10/15° and source-table planetary orbs remain independently labeled **sensitivity policies**; no published human natal scoring is reweighted based on this evidence.
