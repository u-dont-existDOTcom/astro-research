## Original first-edition question

William Lilly's *Christian Astrology*, Book III chapter CVII **printed p.536** (one-based scanned PDF 570), says that **after neither an Ascendant occupant nor a Moon/Mercury joined planet qualifies**, consider the Ascendant lord, **provided its dispositor beholds it by an aspect**. The next instruction turns to a strongly partile aspect or final dignities **“If no Planet aspect him”**. The page's original 1647 printing was directly inspected (page-render SHA-256 **4cdd11d5cf468e20f38fed7889e42f2e6298540e05f7f196c9b916e28bb57b24**; original scanned PDF SHA-256 **2cb53e20e122ffc6e47917ba10241f6c49687cd7a4696f3b5db655a2a1aab28b**; [Wellcome first-print catalogue](https://wellcomecollection.org/works/s7z7c46y)). Original images remain outside public Git.

The historical text does **not** give a complete disposition for every possible astronomical state. This study makes two missing cases visible instead of treating them as an actual author-approved manners ruler.

- **S3, an aspecting distinct dispositor:** the rising-sign classical domicile lord is one planet, its natal domicile dispositor is a different planet, and that dispositor aspects the lord under the stated source interpretation. Lilly's provisional S3 planet is the **Ascendant lord**, *not automatically its dispositor* and not the numerically highest unrelated body.
- **SX, another planet aspects the lord, but its dispositor does not:** the later condition **no planet aspects the lord** is not true, while the required specific dispositor aspect is absent. The original wording does not uniquely appoint a replacement ruler here. The new aggregate identifies every other aspecting classical planet rather than silently making one the governor.
- **SY, the Ascendant lord is its own dispositor:** when the Ascendant lord occupies its own domicile, there is no **distinct dispositor planet** that can behold it by an ordinary two-body ray. Whether Lilly intended that situation to qualify immediately by essential dignity, require some external aspect, or enter another test is historically unadjudicated. A new explicit **SY abstention** protects such cases instead of using absence of a self-aspect as proof of the later S4/S5 conditions.
- **LATER:** neither S1 nor S2 qualifies, the ASC lord has a different dispositor that does not aspect it, and no other traditional planet aspects that lord under the declared source interpretation. Only these distinctly identified cases are provisionally eligible for later partile/essential-place branches without additional self-dispositor ambiguity.

These are *source-conditioned stage diagnoses*. We do not assert that historical interpreters would necessarily adopt the new SY category or that Lilly authorially named an “SX” stage. The new letters mark unresolved source-meaning questions, not newly discovered ancient terminology.
