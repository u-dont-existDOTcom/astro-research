## Gap-directed follow-up

A supplemental interview must address missing behavioral distinctions using neutral prompts chosen from the observed-item and missing-item registries, not from a known natal chart's carriers, favorable scores or desired rank. Ask for actual examples, counterexamples, context, recurrence and changes over time. Examples are not instructions to choose one predetermined answer. In particular:

- Separate naturally developed abilities, unsolicited recognition, motivation from recognition, actual performance, and other people's expectations.
- Compare self-initiation and externally invited collaboration using real cases. Separate the goal's origin from resources that make it feasible.
- Measure work and recovery at defined ordinary versus intense workloads, routine stability, skill repetition, motives for mastery, and investigation versus novelty seeking.
- Distinguish the capacity to influence from frequency of use, communication style from persuasive outcome, and group influence from formal command.
- Separate achievement, competition, status, autonomy, usefulness, control of resources, and willingness to endure meaningful difficulty.
- Distinguish correction of consequential errors from trivial imperfection; rational priorities and preserving commitments from pressure to finish.
- Separate reciprocal cooperation from generosity or noticing needs; emotional resonance from intentional regulation, and stable values from changing circumstances.
- Recover previously pending clarifications, rather than asking again about well-established patterns.
